\begin{frame}{This week}
\small
\begin{enumerate}\small
  \item \textbf{Ran every Tricycle matrix multiplication encrypted on a GPU} through Cerium, at three layer sizes. All outputs correct.
  \item \textbf{Ran the baseline:} Cerium's own encrypted BERT-Base, end to end.
  \item \textbf{Profiled the baseline} per function, to see where its time goes.
  \item \textbf{Cut our matrix-multiplication time} for one BERT-Base layer from 2.81 s to 1.82 s, and compared it with Cerium's, layer for layer.
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{0.6em}
\begin{block}{Headline}
Tricycle's attention multiplications are 1.9$\times$ faster than Cerium's; its weight multiplications are still 2.4--3.8$\times$ slower; 1.6$\times$ slower overall.
\end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Experimental setup}

\begin{frame}{Result 1: Tricycle matrix multiplications are correct on the GPU}
\small
\begin{center}\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrl@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Layer shape} & \textbf{Products} & \textbf{Weight plaintexts} & \textbf{GPU time} & \textbf{Max error}\\
\midrule
Small test & all 7 & 78 & 10.9 ms & $1.6\times10^{-6}$\\
BERT-Tiny & all 7 & 1{,}084 & 210.6 ms & $6.6\times10^{-6}$\\
BERT-Base & all 7 & 10{,}590 & 1.82 s & $2.5\times10^{-5}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.3em}
\begin{itemize}\small
  \item Every encrypted output matches the unencrypted result; output values reach about 5.
  \item At scale $2^{56}$ errors were about $10^{-2}$: each rotation adds noise of about $2^{52}$. Scale $2^{84}$ fixed it.
  \item BERT-Base does not fit in 80 GB as one program (32 GB of weights plus a 66 GB register file), so it runs as four programs; its time is their sum.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{itemize}\small
  \item \textbf{First to now:} Tricycle's own rotation-schedule weights, and the attention output and feed-forward products at Cerium's ciphertext level (5 instead of 12).
  \item \textbf{Remaining gap:} Cerium compresses its weight plaintexts and fuses its rotations; ours multiplies full-size weight plaintexts one at a time.
\end{itemize}

\begin{frame}{Takeaways and next steps}
\small
\begin{block}{Takeaways}
\begin{itemize}\small
  \item All Tricycle matrix multiplications run correctly, encrypted, through Cerium.
  \item Tricycle already beats Cerium's attention multiplications by 1.9$\times$.
  \item Bootstrapping is half of Cerium's BERT time, so faster matrix multiplications alone cannot make a layer more than about 1.5$\times$ faster.
\end{itemize}
\end{block}
\begin{block}{Next}
\begin{itemize}\small
  \item Close the weight-product gap: fused rotations, smaller plaintexts, a lower level for QKV.
  \item Add the nonlinear operations for one complete encrypted Tricycle layer, then compare end to end.
\end{itemize}
\end{block}
\end{frame}
